## Preparation and learning objectives

Read the [capstone workbook](/learn/candidate-nomination/capstone-workbook/), and
its Case 5 on the cross-domain nomination with a missing requirement, and the
[multi-endpoint integration laboratory](/labs/multi-endpoint-integration/)
first. This laboratory executes the rule that case teaches. You will resolve
five required inputs for each candidate, name the input that blocks the
nomination, report the next decision the evidence points to, and see that a
census of endpoint families is not a nomination decision.

## Fresh independent computation

The mode is a fresh independent computation under a frozen ChEMBL 37 protocol.
Use the project's Python 3.11 environment. The first command validates the
existing caches and writes nothing. All commands use no network once the
snapshots are cached and need no accelerator. The export command overwrites
the committed summary only after the contract and summary checks pass.

## The rule this laboratory executes

The rule is the curriculum's, not a laboratory's. The workbook states a
fictional project's teaching thresholds and five required inputs, and this
laboratory runs exactly that rule in one place.

| Required input | The rule | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potency | A matched potency of at most 50 nM | The admitted response-potency points |
| Selectivity ratio | A ratio of at least 20 against an intended target | Not covered by the measured families |
| Exposure ratio | An exposure-to-effective-concentration ratio of at least 3 | Not covered by the measured families |
| Safety package | An independently reviewed safety package | Not covered by the measured families |
| Functional validation | Interpretable functional validation | Not covered by the measured families |

The thresholds are the fictional project's teaching thresholds and not
pharmacological criteria. No composite, no weighted sum, no overall
desirability value and no ordering of the candidates is produced, and the
audit refuses a decision that carries a field the rule does not name, a
missing input filled with a default, or a next decision that points at
changing the product requirement.

## What the teaching table decides

The workbook's own three candidates are executed by the same code, and the
computed answer is the lesson's worked answer.

| Candidate | Blocked by | Next decision the evidence points to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N1 | Selectivity ratio (8 against a required 20) | Revise the candidate |
| N2 | Exposure ratio (2 against a required 3) | Revise the candidate |
| N3 | Safety package missing | Close an evidence gap |

None of the three satisfies the complete requirement, and none of them points
at changing the profile: changing a threshold so that a favourite passes is a
change to the requirement and not a discovery.

## What the measured cohort decides

The measured cohort is every parent the validated integration laboratory reads
that carries at least two measured endpoint families, because a cross-domain
decision cannot be posed on a single family. Of the 7,908 parents in the
cohort, 8 carry only efficacy, 1,062 only exposure, 6,780 only interaction,
1 carries efficacy with exposure and 57 carry exposure with interaction. Those
58 are the measured candidates.

The rule cannot be posed on any of them, and the reason is specific.

| Required input | Measured candidates | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potency | 57 absent, 1 indeterminate | The one candidate with potency points mixes endpoint types, so no single potency value is the family's |
| Selectivity ratio | 58 not covered | A comparison between CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 is not a selectivity ratio against an intended target |
| Exposure ratio | 58 not covered | In vitro intrinsic clearance is not an exposure and cannot form the ratio without a declared effective concentration |
| Safety package | 58 not covered | No measured safety material exists here, and a review is a human decision no measurement supplies |
| Functional validation | 58 not covered | The measured assay records classify assay records rather than validate a compound's function |

All 58 are blocked at the potency input, all point at closing an evidence gap,
and none completes a nomination. This is the measured form of the point the
endpoint-counting laboratory cannot make: a census that reports 7,908
compounds, 1,120 exposure medians and 6,837 CYP inhibition measurements
describes coverage of the measured families, and says nothing about whether
the inputs a nomination decision needs are among them. Here they are not. The
two families the cohort holds in abundance are not the two the rule asks for.

## Quantitative uncertainty-aware EGFR nomination audit (`quant-tradeoff`)

When target-matched replicates, selectivity, exposure intervals, and synthesis
yields are assembled for `4` real ChEMBL 37 EGFR candidates (`4` EGFR IC50
replicates each; thresholds `<= 50.0` nM potency, `>= 20.0`-fold selectivity vs
ERBB2 `CHEMBL1824`, `>= 3.0`-fold exposure ratio, and `>= 0.5` route yield),
running `quant-tradeoff` and `check-quant-tradeoff` shows how conservative 95%
confidence bounds change the nomination outcome relative to point estimates (`2`
point-rule survivors vs `1` conservative 95% CI survivor):

```bash
.venv/bin/python labs/crossdomain_tradeoff/inspect_crossdomain_tradeoff.py quant-tradeoff
.venv/bin/python labs/crossdomain_tradeoff/inspect_crossdomain_tradeoff.py check-quant-tradeoff
```

- **`CHEMBL553` (`Erlotinib`)**: EGFR IC50 median `2.1` nM (95% CI `[1.4, 2.6]`
  nM), ERBB2 (`1450.0` nM) selectivity `690.4762`-fold (95% lower bound
  `500.0`-fold), exposure ratio `18.5`-fold (`[14.2, 22.8]`-fold), `1`-step SNAr
  yield `0.78`, passes both point and 95% CI rules.
- **`CHEMBL939` (`Gefitinib`)**: EGFR IC50 median `22.5` nM (`[15.0, 33.0]` nM),
  ERBB2 (`1800.0` nM) selectivity `80.0`-fold (`54.5455`-fold lower bound),
  `1`-step SNAr yield `0.81`, passes the point exposure ratio (`3.4 >= 3.0`) but
  is blocked under the conservative 95% lower-bound exposure rule (`2.2 < 3.0`,
  95% CI `[2.2, 4.6]`-fold).
- **`CHEMBL1201583` (`Lapatinib`)**: EGFR IC50 median `11.1` nM (`[9.2, 14.0]`
  nM), exposure ratio `4.1`-fold (`[3.1, 5.2]`-fold), `3`-step route yield `0.42`,
  blocked at `selectivity_ratio` (`0.8108`-fold, 95% lower bound `0.6429`-fold
  against ERBB2 `9.0` nM).
- **`CHEMBL53711` (`PD-153035`)**: EGFR IC50 median `0.032` nM (`[0.025, 0.05]`
  nM), selectivity `450.0`-fold (`310.0`-fold lower bound against ERBB2 `14.4` nM),
  `1`-step yield `0.84`, blocked at `exposure_ratio` (`0.8`-fold, 95% CI
  `[0.4, 1.2]`-fold).

## Independent exercise

Inspect `CHEMBL1144`, the only measured candidate with potency points. Name
the state of each of the five inputs, state which input blocks the
nomination, and explain why a selectivity ratio cannot be formed from the
cohort's CYP inhibition records even when both enzymes are measured.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

Its potency input is indeterminate: the admitted records mix endpoint types,
so the family has no single potency value and the gate cannot be decided. Its
selectivity ratio, exposure ratio, safety package and functional validation
are each not covered, for the reasons in the table above. The potency input
therefore blocks the nomination, the next decision is to close an evidence
gap, and the nomination is not complete. A selectivity ratio compares a
compound's activity against two drug-metabolising enzymes. The workbook's
input is a ratio against an intended target, and a comparison between two
metabolism enzymes does not establish anything about the target the compound
is meant to act on, however clean the numbers are.

</details>
