## Before you run

**Mode: runnable inspection of a constructed record.** The command reads one
committed JSON file and matches substructures with RDKit. **No outcome here is a
real result**, no compound was tested, and **nothing in this lab is a safety
assessment of any substance.**

```bash
python labs/safety_evidence/inspect_safety.py show
```

A tox profile prints five different kinds of evidence in one column, and they are
read as one gradient of badness. They are obtained five different ways and can be
wrong about five different things.

## 1. The record never stores an alert

The file stores findings that **required an experiment**: an assay outcome, an
organ liability, an organism-level observation. Alerts are computed from the
structure every time and stored nowhere.

That is the whole design decision. Storing an alert beside a measured outcome is
the collapse this lab is about, so a check refuses a record that does it and a
known-bad test performs it to prove the check fires.

## 2. Most of the alerting compounds were never measured

**7 of 12** compounds match at least one alert. **5 of those carry no measured
finding at all.**

Their entire safety record is a pattern match. Nobody ran anything. On a profile
page that column looks exactly like the column next to it, which holds results.

One compound matches **two** alerts while carrying no finding. The alert count
rose. The evidence did not.

## 3. The levels disagree, and two of the disagreements are different

| situation | compounds |
| --- | --- |
| alert fired, a measurement said negative | `c01` |
| alert fired, nobody measured anything | `c03` `c04` `c05` `c06` `c07` |
| no alert, something measured positive | `c08` `c09` |

The last row kills the reassuring direction: **the absence of an alert predicted
nothing.** Two compounds with a clean structural profile carry a positive finding.

The first two rows look alike on a report and are not alike at all. **An alert a
measurement contradicted has been tested and lost. An alert nobody checked has
not been tested.** Printing them the same way is how an alert keeps authority it
never earned.

## 4. Without a dose context none of it is a risk statement

**2 of 7** assay outcomes carry no concentration, so they cannot be compared with
the ones that do. The recorded concentrations span a factor of four.

**No exposure is declared anywhere in the record**, and the file says why that
matters rather than leaving the gap silent. A positive at 200 micromolar and a
positive at 1 micromolar read the same on a profile page and mean entirely
different things.

## 5. The level that would settle it is the one almost nothing has

**1 of 12** compounds carries any organism-level evidence.

That is the ordinary shape of a safety record, not a defect in this one. Alerts
are free, assays are cheap, organisms are expensive, and a profile page gives all
three the same amount of space.

```bash
python labs/safety_evidence/inspect_safety.py check
```

Fourteen checks run. A record that cannot be read as five separated levels is not
summarised at all, because a summary of a collapsed record would be the thing this
lab warns against.

## Independent exercise

A candidate review slide reads: "compound shows two structural alerts and a
positive hERG signal. Recommend deprioritising in favour of the backup series,
which is clean."

Say what each half of that sentence rests on, and name the one question that
would change the decision.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer: compare evidence at matched exposure and coverage</summary>

The two structural alerts identify computed substructure matches. They are
evidence about the representation, not measurements of toxicity. Multiple alerts
can refer to the same structural feature, so their count is not a count of
independent hazards. The constructed example above includes an alert-positive
compound without a recorded finding; it does not show that compound to be safe.

The stated hERG signal needs its assay format, concentration-response evidence,
quality controls and relation to relevant exposure. A signal at 100 micromolar
and one at 1 micromolar cannot be treated as equivalent merely because both are
called positive. The slide alone does not establish either a valid measurement
or its consequence for the proposed use.

The backup's description as clean needs the same scrutiny. No alert, no observed
finding and no measurement are different states. Compare the endpoints tested,
concentrations, exposure margins and missing results for both series before
ranking them.

Ask which endpoints were measured in the backup, at what concentrations and
under which controls, relative to the lead. Less testing can explain fewer
findings, but does not establish that this is the explanation here. Matched
evidence may preserve, weaken or reverse the proposed ranking. It is not
guaranteed to reverse it.

</details>

## What this does not do

It assesses nothing. No compound here was tested, no outcome is real, and no
statement about any substance can be taken from this file.

It also does not say alerts are useless. **An alert is a cheap prompt to look, and
looking is what the other four levels are.** The failure is printing a prompt to
look in the same column as the result of having looked.

For the surrounding argument see
[metabolism to safety](/learn/admet-pk/metabolism-to-safety/) and

## Real-data clinical safety and unbound hERG exposure-margin audit (`public-safety`)

Beyond the 12-compound constructed record, run the real-data evaluation across `6` clinical drugs from ChEMBL 37 (human hERG `CHEMBL240` and EGFR `CHEMBL203`) and FDA clinical pharmacology reviews:

```bash
.venv/bin/python labs/safety_evidence/inspect_safety.py public-safety
.venv/bin/python labs/safety_evidence/inspect_safety.py check-public-safety
```

- **Alert vs exposure-normalized margin across `6` clinical drugs**:
  - `CHEMBL553` (`Erlotinib`, `150` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.19 uM`, `fu = 0.07`): `1` alert (`terminal_alkyne_cyp_inactivator`), EGFR `IC50 = 0.002 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 28.5 uM`, unbound hERG margin `150.0`x (`log10_herg_margin = 2.1761`), hERG/EGFR selectivity `14250.0`x; clinical DILI monitoring without Torsades boxed warning.
  - `CHEMBL939` (`Gefitinib`, `250` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.035 uM`, `fu = 0.09`): `1` alert (`halogenated_aniline`), EGFR `IC50 = 0.033 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 1.0 uM`, unbound hERG margin `28.57`x (`log10_herg_margin = 1.4559`), hERG/EGFR selectivity `30.3`x.
  - `CHEMBL1201583` (`Lapatinib`, `1250` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.042 uM`, `fu = 0.01`): `1` alert (`para_alkoxy_or_hydroxy_aniline_quinone_imine_precursor`), EGFR `IC50 = 0.011 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 1.1 uM`, unbound hERG margin `26.19`x (`log10_herg_margin = 1.4181`), hERG/EGFR selectivity `100.0`x; FDA Boxed Warning for hepatotoxicity (`HLA-DQA1*02:01` + quinone-imine reactive metabolite).
  - `CHEMBL24828` (`Vandetanib`, `300` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.18 uM`, `fu = 0.06`): `0` alerts, EGFR `IC50 = 0.5 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 0.4 uM`, unbound hERG margin `2.22`x (`log10_herg_margin = 0.3468`, `< 10`x threshold), hERG/EGFR selectivity `0.8`x; FDA Boxed Warning and REMS for Torsades de Pointes.
  - `CHEMBL3353410` (`Osimertinib`, `80` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.025 uM`, `fu = 0.05`): `2` alerts (`michael_acceptor_acrylamide`, `para_alkoxy_or_hydroxy_aniline_quinone_imine_precursor`), EGFR `IC50 = 0.001 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 1.58 uM`, unbound hERG margin `63.2`x (`log10_herg_margin = 1.8007`, `>= 30`x Redfern threshold), hERG/EGFR selectivity `1580.0`x; no Torsades boxed warning.
  - `CHEMBL17157` (`Terfenadine`, `60` mg QD, `Cmax,u = 0.03 uM`, `fu = 0.03`): `0` alerts, EGFR `IC50 = 50.0 uM`, hERG `IC50 = 0.056 uM`, unbound hERG margin `1.87`x (`log10_herg_margin = 0.2711`, `< 10`x threshold), hERG/EGFR selectivity `0.0`x; withdrawn for fatal Torsades de Pointes.
- **Torsades rule comparison**: `structural_alert_only` yields `tp = 0`, `fp = 4`, `fn = 2`, whereas `exposure_linked_herg_margin_under_10x` (`IC50_hERG / Cmax_u < 10x`) yields `tp = 2`, `fp = 0`, `fn = 0`.
