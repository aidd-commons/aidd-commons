Read [the decision-unit lesson](/learn/foundations/decision-unit-scope/) and [generalization unit and estimand](/learn/evaluation-and-reproducibility/generalization-unit-and-estimand/) first. You will distinguish a reported row from an admitted independent measurement, count the values available under each candidate unit, and explain why the unit must accompany any generalization claim. ## Measurement and unit rule

The declared cohort contains 15 seed molecules resolving to 10 parents. The decision-unit admission rule retains nanomolar point values for AC50, EC50, IC50, Kd or Ki after its ordered exclusions. It yields 488 point rows. Rows with the same parent, assay, document and endpoint type collapse to 486 independent measurements. Bounds do not enter this count. The audit assigns each measurement to every candidate value present on its source rows, then counts measurements per value. First and third quartiles use inclusive linear interpolation. The count of values with exactly 1 measurement identifies values that lose their only same-value observation when that measurement is left out.

## Two-unit comparison

Assay and parent molecule differ most in distinct value count under the declared candidates. Both views contain the same 486 measurements. The assay view has 481 values, a median of 1 measurement per value, Q1–Q3 of 1–1, and 476 singleton values. The parent view has 9 values, a median of 27 measurements per value, Q1–Q3 of 4–51, and 1 singleton value. A set of rows is therefore not a count of independent parents. These counts describe the available cohort. They do not choose a split or establish performance on unseen units.

## Limits of the material

The seeds were deliberately selected to exercise the admission rule. Their unit distribution is not an estimate for ChEMBL as a whole. A scaffold is a structural grouping rule, not a complete measure of analogue relatedness. An assay ID does not reveal all laboratory conditions. The table cannot support time, site or patient generalization. The laboratory does not fit a model, score a prediction or declare one unit correct. A leave-one-unit-out split would, by design, leave every held-out unit without same-unit training observations. The singleton count instead describes leave-one-measurement-out at the value level.

## Independent inspection task

The table has 487 molecule-record memberships but 486 grouped measurements. Explain why that is not an arithmetic defect. Choose assay or document as a hypothetical held-out axis and state the question that changes relative to parent holdout.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Check the reasoning after your attempt</summary>

A grouped measurement can carry more than one source molecule-record identifier, so membership counts need not equal the number of unique measurements. Holding out assay or document changes the context of generalization; it does not follow from a successful parent-held-out result. The grouping rule counts units but does not independently establish biological independence.

</details>
