## Learning goals and prerequisites

You will formulate a mechanism hypothesis with an explicit competing model,
describe what CRISPR and Perturb-seq observe, and separate target evidence from
tractability and risk. Read [Disease to target](/learn/mechanism-targets/disease-to-target/)
and [Genetics to target evidence](/learn/mechanism-targets/genetics-to-target-evidence/)
first. This conceptual lesson provides no gene-editing conditions, guide design,
cell culture steps, or clinical recommendation.

## A mechanism claim has an object, a contrast, and a losing alternative

Let `X` be a defined perturbation, `C` a cell context, `Y` a measured endpoint,
and `H` a stated route from `X` to `Y`. A compact hypothesis is: “In context C,
changing X changes Y through mediator M.” The required counterevidence is not
an afterthought. Name an alternative `H_alt`, such as generic stress, altered
cell composition, an off-target perturbation, assay interference, or a pathway
parallel to M. Then select a contrast whose expected outputs differ under `H`
and `H_alt`.

An observed difference `Δ = mean(Y | X, C) − mean(Y | control, C)` is a
descriptive estimand only after the comparison unit, controls, aggregation, and
normalization are declared. It is not automatically a causal effect of the
intended gene. Technical single cells are measurements nested within a
perturbation, sample, and context; treating every cell as an independent
organism or patient inflates certainty. Pooled designs additionally require a
guide identity and assignment-quality contract.

## What perturbational readouts do—and do not—validate

CRISPR perturbation changes a genomic locus or its expression according to the
chosen system; it does not itself name the molecular mediator. A bulk screen can
report enrichment or depletion in a selected population. Perturb-seq associates
an assigned perturbation with a high-dimensional single-cell RNA readout. It can
make the response pattern and cell-state heterogeneity inspectable, but an RNA
signature is neither direct protein activity nor proof that another context will
respond. Genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq has been demonstrated at over 2.5
million cells; scale changes the evidence available, not the causal meaning of
an untested alternative [Replogle et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35688146/).

For a prespecified gene set `G`, one transparent summary is a signed module
score `S_i = (1/|G|) Σ_{g∈G} z_{ig}`, where `z_{ig}` is a declared normalized
expression value for gene `g` in cell `i`. `S_i` is unitless on that scale.
Compare distributions of `S_i` across assigned perturbations, but also inspect
guide agreement, viable-cell representation, and whether the same change occurs
in a negative-control perturbation. A significant score is evidence about that
score; it is not a target-validation certificate.

## Tractability is a separate decision

Target validity asks whether changing a biological object changes a relevant
outcome under a defined mechanism. Tractability asks whether a modality could
make a sufficiently selective, exposure-compatible intervention. Risk includes
expression and pathway roles in non-target contexts, homologous proteins,
genetic human evidence, and uncertainty that could defeat the product profile.
Thus a target can have persuasive perturbation evidence and still be a poor
programme choice; conversely, a tractable binder without causal disease evidence
is not a validated target. Keep those evidence columns separate.

## Worked case: preserve the counterexample

This is a **constructed** example. In a named model context C1, a gene-silencing
perturbation `X` yields a mean pathway score of `−0.60` across three independent
preparations; the matched non-targeting control mean is `−0.10`. Therefore
`Δ = −0.60 − (−0.10) = −0.50` score units. Two independently specified guides
show the same direction. This supports “X is associated with lower score in C1”;
it does not establish that the desired target mechanism caused the score.

The team predicts `H`: X lowers the score through mediator M. It registers a
rescue comparison that restores M without changing the broader experimental
context. The constructed rescue mean is `−0.16`, closer to the control. A
separate generic-stress perturbation produces `−0.55`. The rescue is consistent
with H, while the stress result keeps `H_alt` plausible. The honest next line is
not “M is validated”: it is “the observed score may be M-linked but is not yet
specific to M; an orthogonal, target-proximal measurement and a second relevant
context are required.”

For tractability, suppose the same constructed evidence table records an
accessible binding pocket but high expression in a non-target tissue and no
selectivity assessment against a homologous family. The target is **not**
discarded or promoted from that table. It receives “opportunity present; safety
and selectivity risks unresolved.” The missing column matters as much as the
favourable one.

## Alternatives, limitations, and independent exercise

An isogenic rescue, a second perturbation modality, an orthogonal protein-level
readout, or a different disease-relevant context may be useful because each
changes a different link in the causal chain. None is universal. A rescue can be
misexpressed; a second guide can share a systematic artifact; a protein readout
may still be downstream. Causal evidence accumulates when contrasts predictably
separate H from credible alternatives, not by accumulating correlated assays.

**Handworked exercise (not runnable or empirical):** In constructed context C2,
the control mean module score is `0.12`; perturbation Q has `−0.18`; a distinct
guide for Q has `−0.16`; and a generic growth-slowing control has `−0.20`.
Calculate both Q contrasts. Write one supported sentence, name the strongest
remaining alternative, and choose one next measurement that could distinguish it
from a Q-specific mechanism. Which of “causal target,” “tractable modality,” and
“context-specific perturbation response” is currently supported?

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The contrasts are `−0.18 − 0.12 = −0.30` and `−0.16 − 0.12 = −0.28` score
units. A supported sentence is: “Two constructed Q guides were associated with
lower module score in C2.” Generic growth slowing remains a strong alternative
because its score is `−0.20`. A target-proximal readout or an isogenic rescue
that makes different predictions under the two explanations could help. Only a
context-specific perturbation response is supported; causal target and tractable
modality remain unestablished.

</details>
