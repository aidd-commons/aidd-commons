## Learning goals

After this lesson, you should be able to write the input and target for a
context-conditioned perturbation task, compare it with a condition-mean and
additive control, and name the cell, donor, and context units behind a
generalization statement. You should also be able to separate transferred
prediction from causal target validation.

## Prerequisites and task boundary

Read [From disease question to target hypothesis](/learn/mechanism-targets/disease-to-target/)
and [Biological representations](/learn/representations/biological-representations/)
first. Let `X` be an unperturbed cellular state, `A` a declared perturbation,
`C` a context descriptor, and `Y` a post-perturbation expression profile or
other readout. The task must state whether it predicts a full distribution
`P(Y | X, A, C)`, a mean `E[Y | X, A, C]`, or a change relative to an appropriate
control. Here `X` can represent an unperturbed population or condition; it does
not imply that the same cell is measured before and after a perturbation.

In a pooled single-cell experiment, cells may be observations while donor,
cell line, tissue, time, stimulation, and culture protocol define contexts.
The independent unit for a claim must be declared rather than inferred from a
large cell count. The original [Perturb-seq article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5181115/)
links CRISPR perturbation identities to single-cell RNA profiles in a pooled
setting; it does not make every observed expression association a general causal
conclusion.

## Training objective and target semantics

Single-cell expression measurement is typically destructive. Baseline and
post-perturbation cells are therefore usually unpaired populations, not paired
before--after observations of one cell. A condition-mean target can be written
as `Ybar(c, a)`, the mean over eligible cells from a declared condition and
perturbation. A mean model could minimize, on training conditions only,
`L_mean = sum_(c,a in T_train) ||mu_hat(c,a) - Ybar(c,a)||^2`.

This differs from predicting a distribution. A distributional target must state
how the observed cell population defines `P_hat(Y | c, a)` and use a declared
likelihood or distributional distance. A model can match a condition mean while
missing variance, multimodality, or a changing cell-state mixture. The loss,
aggregation rule, and independent unit must therefore match the claimed target.

Learned predictor/control parameters and perturbation-effect estimates are fit
on training data only. An explicitly allowed new-context unperturbed baseline
may be summarized at inference, never its held-out post-perturbation response.

## PIE: name the effect and the held-out axis

The [PIE within-dataset release](https://huggingface.co/arcinstitute/PIE_replogle_wdataset), checked on 2026-10-25, makes this population target explicit. Perceiver IO combines knowledge embeddings, baseline control means and training response evidence. For each context–perturbation pair it predicts gene-wise DE probability (`p_de`), log2 fold change (`lfc_pred`) and change in processed mean expression (`delta_p_pred`). These are distinct quantities. None is the before-and-after trajectory of an individual cell, and a mean change does not determine a full expression distribution. The [processed-data definition](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arcinstitute/PIE_replogle_nadig_essential) makes control choice, filtering and statistical thresholds part of the target.

Its [canonical splits](https://huggingface.co/datasets/arcinstitute/PIE_splits) distinguish `unseen_ctx`, `unseen_pert` and `unseen_ctx_pert`. The 4 released checkpoint folders name the held-out cell line; they represent separate held-out-context folds, not an ensemble. A held-out-context test can allow its unperturbed baseline without allowing its perturbation responses. The [single-cell model reading](/learn/representations/single-cell-and-virtual-cell-models/) separates the release's code, checkpoint and data terms. No PIE model was run here.

### Constructed holdout exercise

Training responses cover `(Alpha, A)`, `(Alpha, B)`, `(Beta, A)` and `(Beta, B)`. Gamma has only an allowed unperturbed baseline; perturbation C has no training response. Classify these proposed test pairs using the released split vocabulary:

| Query | Held-out axis |
| --- | --- |
| `(Gamma, A)` | Context only: `unseen_ctx` |
| `(Alpha, C)` | Perturbation only: `unseen_pert` |
| `(Gamma, C)` | Both: `unseen_ctx_pert` |

Would `(Alpha, A)` support any of those claims if its training cells were split again into a test file? Could the baseline for Gamma supply its perturbation-effect label? This is a constructed partition exercise, not PIE data or a measured result.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

`(Alpha, A)` holds out neither context nor perturbation, even if its cells occur in a different file. Gamma's unperturbed mean supplies baseline information only; using its perturbed response to form an effect label during fitting would cross the holdout. The three rows test different kinds of transfer and must be reported separately. Counting more cells within a held-out line does not create more independent held-out cell lines, donors or tissues.

</details>

## Representation, controls, and intervention labels

A useful input contract records how `X` was measured, how `A` was assigned or
delivered, which covariates enter `C`, and the intended target. A condition-mean
control predicts the observed mean for the matching context. A simple additive
control writes
`mu_hat(C, A) = mu_hat(C, control) + delta_hat(A)`.
It assumes the perturbation shift is shared across contexts. That is a useful
baseline precisely because a context-specific response can violate it. Its
condition and perturbation estimates must be fitted on the training partition
only.

An intervention label records an intended manipulation, such as a specified
guide or compound exposure. An observational label records a naturally observed
state or association. Intervention labels are stronger evidence about what was
assigned, but guide efficiency, off-target effects, selection, delivery, and
measurement still need their own checks. A model trained on either type does
not automatically establish a causal target mechanism.

## Worked case: an average effect hides a sign change

The following table is a **constructed teaching example**. Each number is a
condition mean for one readout; it is not a reported biological result.

| Context | Mean control | Mean after perturbation `A` | Change |
| ------- | -----------: | --------------------------: | -----: |
| Alpha   |           10 |                          14 |   `+4` |
| Beta    |           10 |                           6 |   `-4` |

With equal weighting, the descriptive average perturbation change across the
displayed Alpha and Beta values is zero. It is not a legitimate held-out Beta
prediction because it has used Beta's response. If Alpha alone were training
data, an additive control would estimate `delta_hat(A) = +4` and, given Beta's
allowed baseline mean of 10, predict 14 for Beta. It would fail the constructed
Beta response of 6. The lesson is not that every perturbation has a sign change.
It is that averaging can erase the interaction between context and perturbation,
so a task must say which contexts are held out and which quantity is evaluated.

The [2026 Virtual Cell Challenge](https://arcinstitute.org/news/virtual-cell-challenge-2026)
describes a zero-shot task in which models predict CRISPRi responses in cell
contexts that have not been seen perturbed. It supports the relevance of an
unseen-context task definition. It does not supply contest outcomes, a universal
metric, or causal validation for a predicted response.

## Worked case, on real data: does context reverse a real signature?

The Alpha/Beta table above is invented. The [LINCS L1000 perturbation-
context lab](/labs/l1000-perturbation-context/) asks the same question on
real measurements: reading GEO series
[GSE70138](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE70138)
directly, for 1,721 real small-molecule compounds measured in two real
cell lines, MCF7 and PC3, at a fixed dose and timepoint, how well does a
compound's transcriptional signature in one cell line predict its
signature in the other?

On the raw measured expression values, matched compound pairs have median
Spearman rho 0.79 and mismatched pairs 0.70. Subtracting each profile's
plate-matched vehicle-control baseline changes those medians to 0.175 and
0.034. The reported two-sided Mann-Whitney U value is p = 7.9 × 10⁻⁸²;
its interpretation depends on dependence between the compared profiles.
About one-fifth of the 1,721 compounds have negative cross-cell-line
correlation after correction. This is a negative association between
ranked gene-response patterns, not proof that each gene or the whole
response reverses sign as in the scalar Alpha/Beta example.

**Independent exercise (using the retained lab output):** Explain what
plate-matched baseline subtraction and the fixed null rotation each test.
Why is neither equivalent to holding out Beta or fitting a shared
`delta_hat(A)`? Explain why negative cross-gene correlation is different
from a scalar sign reversal.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

Plate-matched subtraction defines the response relative to a local baseline.
The fixed null rotation compares matched compounds with one deterministic
mismatch. Neither operation fits an additive predictor or creates a
held-out-context test. The raw medians of 0.79 and 0.70 show why absolute
expression agreement alone cannot identify compound-specific agreement;
the corrected comparison asks a different, descriptive question.

</details>

## Inference and evaluation boundary

At inference, provide the baseline state and perturbation under the same input
contract used for training. If the intended setting is a new donor or a new cell
line, hold it out at the relevant level rather than only withholding cells from
the same source. Report whether an error or distributional score aggregates over
cells, donors, perturbations, contexts, or a hierarchy of those units.

A transferred prediction can be useful when it is evaluated in an unseen,
declared context. It remains a predictive statement. Causal target validation
requires an appropriate intervention and readout, plus controls that distinguish
the target mechanism from alternative mechanisms. Prediction transfer and causal
validation answer related but non-interchangeable questions.

## Independent exercise

Add a third context, Gamma, with mean control 8 and mean after perturbation
`A` equal to 12. State the three context-specific changes. Would an additive
control with one shared `delta_hat(A)` represent all three changes exactly? If
Beta is held out, may its post-perturbation mean contribute to `delta_hat(A)`?
Name the unit you would hold out to claim generalization to a new donor.

<details data-worked-answer>
<summary>Worked answer</summary>

The changes are Alpha `+4`, Beta `-4`, and Gamma `+4`. No one shared additive
shift represents all three exactly because Beta has the opposite sign. A donor
generalization claim should hold out donors, not merely cells sampled from the
same donor. Beta's post-perturbation mean must not contribute to a training-only
`delta_hat(A)`. Cell-level predictions can still be evaluated, but they do not
turn cells from one donor into independent donor-level contexts.

</details>

## Failure boundaries and source path

Do not call a model causal because it was trained on perturbation labels, and do
not call a correct-looking condition mean transferable because the cell count is
large. Record intervention identity, delivery and timing, baseline definition,
context variables, mean or distribution target, training-only fitting boundary,
held-out unit, and the control before interpreting an output.
