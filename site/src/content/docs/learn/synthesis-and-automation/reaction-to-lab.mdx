## Foundations

Reaction prediction, atom mapping, retrosynthesis, and laboratory automation
make different claims. A route proposal must still survive material
availability, protecting-group choices, safety, scale, purification, and
observed yield.

## Practical workflow

Define the target molecule, allowed starting materials, operational
constraints, route budget, and what counts as a laboratory success. Use an
atom-mapping or reaction model as one evidence stream, then ask a chemist to
inspect the proposed transformations. [RXNMapper](https://github.com/rxn4chemistry/rxnmapper)
and [AiZynthFinder](https://github.com/MolecularAI/aizynthfinder) document
different parts of that chain.

## Evaluation

Keep retrospective route recovery separate from prospective execution. Record
the reaction conditions, material provenance, outcome, purification burden,
and failed routes. A reaction data split should prevent the benchmark from
leaking near-identical transformations.

## Frontier and open questions

[Chemformer](https://github.com/MolecularAI/Chemformer),
[ASKCOS](https://github.com/ASKCOS/askcos), and
[RetroSim](https://github.com/connorcoley/retrosim) offer distinct modelling or
retrieval choices. Integration with laboratory automation remains an
experimental systems problem rather than a score-optimization exercise.

## A bounded worked decision and independent task

After [molecular representations](/learn/representations/small-molecule-representations/), distinguish a graph operation from an observed reaction. The authored [p01 retrosynthesis case](/labs/retro-disconnection/) records product CC(=O)Nc1ccccc1 and reactants CC(=O)O plus Nc1ccccc1. Recovering those graphs matches the recorded disconnection. No reaction conditions, yield or laboratory feasibility was observed. To inspect real condition-resolved reaction records (`12` Perera et al. `2018` Suzuki-Miyaura records spanning `0.0000` to `0.9029` yield on `1` shared biaryl product and `24` Ahneman et al. `2018` Buchwald-Hartwig `1536`-well records spanning `0.00%` to `98.73%`), USPTO atom maps, AiZynthFinder route trees, and closed-loop HTE feedback gating (`27.19%` substrate-only MAE vs `16.51%` condition-aware MAE), run `python labs/retro_disconnection/inspect_disconnection.py public-records` and `python labs/retro_disconnection/inspect_disconnection.py check-public`.

A planning tool returns those reactants and a successful software status. Which success occurred, and what feedback is still needed before calling the route experimentally successful?

The software returned a graph proposal under its declared rule. Experimental success would need an authorized reaction record, materials and conditions, interpretable measurement, and the stated product/quality criterion. A successful return is not an observed yield, and a failed detector readout is not necessarily failed synthesis. Continue to [reaction mapping and route search](/learn/synthesis-and-automation/reaction-mapping-and-route-search/) for the mechanism and evaluation boundary.

## What v0.1 does not yet teach

v0.1 does not certify synthetic routes, provide laboratory safety procedures,
or assert that a predicted route will work at a particular scale.

## Core-source path

Read the [ORD specification](https://open-reaction-database.org/) to see how
reaction records are represented, then inspect
[AiZynthFinder](https://github.com/MolecularAI/aizynthfinder) and
[ASKCOS](https://github.com/ASKCOS/askcos) with the proposed route and
experimental constraints in view.
